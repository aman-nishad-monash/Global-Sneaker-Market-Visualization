Five countries ship 74% of the world's athletic shoes

Top five exporters and importers of athletic-style footwear, by trade value (US$).

68.8→74.4%: The top five keep getting bigger

Five countries sold 68.8% of the world's athletic shoes in 2002. By 2024 it was 74.4%.

World exports tripled, from US$44 billion to US$132 billion. The biggest exporters grew faster than everyone else.

×10.7: Vietnam grew nearly eleven times over

Vietnam's exports rose from US$2.9 billion to US$31.2 billion.

China's doubled, from US$20.7 billion to US$41.7 billion. But the world tripled, so China's share still fell.

18.6%: One country buys nearly a fifth

The United States bought US$24.5 billion of shoes in 2024. That is 18.6% of all imports.

It is more than Germany and France combined (US$19.4 billion).

The biggest flows start in Asia and end in Europe or North America

The 15 largest footwear trade flows between world regions (US$).

2023: Southeast Asia overtook East Asia

In 2023 Southeast Asia exported US$43.3 billion against East Asia's US$42.4 billion. A year later the lead was US$4.6 billion.

In 2002 East Asia held 50% of the world's exports and Southeast Asia only 14%.

20 of 147: Few countries sell more than they buy

Only 20 of 147 countries with data are net exporters.

China and Vietnam together sell US$66.9 billion more than they buy. That is 73% of all 20 countries' surplus.

42%: Europe mostly buys from Europe

Of the US$62.3 billion of shoes Europe imported in 2024, US$26.0 billion (42%) came from other European countries.

The flow map leaves this out. It shows only trade between regions.

63%: Two companies take most of the money

Nike and adidas earned US$71.9 billion in 2024. That is 63% of the eight companies' US$113.4 billion.

The other six share the remaining 37%.

×2.0: ANTA doubled its sales

ANTA grew from US$4.9 billion to US$9.8 billion between 2019 and 2024, about 15% a year.

Over the same years Nike grew 24%. adidas ended about level (US$26.5 billion to US$25.6 billion), though it sold Reebok in between.

−9.8%: Nike shrank, then left the S&P 100

Revenue fell from US$51.4 billion to US$46.3 billion.

It then stalled at US$46.4 billion.

Nike left the S&P 100 on 21 September 2026.

Nike's Greater China sales fell 11% while North America grew

Nike brand revenue by region (left) and product (right), fiscal 2026, US$, with the change on fiscal 2025.

79%: China's fall erased most of North America's gain

North America added US$939 million. Greater China lost US$739 million.

The loss wipes out 79% of that gain.

Reports state that, it is due to heavy competition from ANTA and Deckers brands catching important shelf space in stores.

65%: Shoes are two-thirds of Nike, and flat

Footwear brought in US$29.5 billion, 65% of Nike brand revenue, and grew just 0.05%.

Clothing grew 3.7%. Growth now comes from apparel, not shoes.

45%: North America is the anchor

North America brings in US$20.5 billion, 45% of Nike brand revenue.

That is more than Europe, the Middle East, Africa and Greater China combined (US$18.4 billion).

Nike and adidas earn most of the money. But a collector does not pay the shop price. What happens to a shoe once it sells out?

Jordan sells easily and holds its value; adidas sells easily but loses it

Ten brands. Across: pairs the average shoe sold in the year to August 2020. Up: typical resale price compared with retail. Size: how many models the brand has.

How to read: bubble size is how many models the brand has in the data, so Nike, with the most shoes, has the biggest bubble. Right of the dashed vertical line, a brand's shoes sell more pairs than the average shoe in the data. Above the dashed horizontal line, a typical shoe resells for more than it cost in the shop.

201 against 81: Jordan sells more per shoe

A typical Jordan model sold 201 pairs and resells 7.1% above retail. A typical Nike model sold 81 pairs and sits 4.2% below retail.

Jordan's 2,335 models sold 468,674 pairs. Nike's 5,407 models sold 438,821.

−28.6%: adidas sells easily but loses value

The average adidas model sold 173 pairs, more than twice Nike's 81. Yet the typical adidas model resells 28.6% below retail.

Selling easily is not the same as holding value.

69%: The Nike group runs StockX

Nike, Jordan and Converse make up 67% of models and 69% of the pairs sold in the year to August 2020.

Out of 1.38 million pairs.

The typical Nike shoe resells 4.2% below retail. But a typical value hides a split. Look at every Nike model.

Most Nike shoes lose money on resale, but the few that win, win big

Nike models grouped by how their typical resale price compares with retail (StockX, 2012 to 2020).

52.5%: More than half lose money

2,839 of Nike's 5,407 models typically resell below retail, by 25% in the middle, which is about $38 a pair.

These shoes also account for 52% of the pairs Nike sold in the year to August 2020, so the losers are not just rare shoes.

+$150: The winners win big

1,007 models resell at least 50% above retail, gaining $150 a pair in the middle. Over 10.6% of models resell for more than double.

A shoe that sells above retail usually sells far above it.

What the winners share

Collaborations are 22% of the models that more than double in value.

Winners were also cheaper in the shop: $130 in the middle, against $150 for the losers.

Selling easily is one thing. What do collectors pay compared with the shop price?

Collaboration shoes lost most of their resale premium after 2013

Typical resale price compared with retail (shop) price.

A collaboration is a shoe made with a partner, such as Off-White.

92→18: The collaboration gap shrank

In early 2013, collaborations resold 92 percentage points above ordinary shoes.

By late 2019 the gap was 18 points.

+7.5%: Collaborations never fell below retail

Their lowest typical price was +7.5% above retail, in the second quarter of 2019.

Ordinary models bottomed at −11.2% in the third quarter of 2019.

This shows the value buyers give to collaboration shoes.

Older shoes kept their value; newer ones fell below retail

From 2019 to January–July 2020, the typical price rose for every release year, by 22 percentage points on average.

0 of 54: Old shoes never sold below retail

None of the 54 squares for shoes released from 2007 to 2012 is below retail.

Roughly 56% of models sit below the "resale = retail" line. About 35% sit between the two lines, and about 9% above the double line.

Most shoes resell near or below retail. So which few shoe lines make real money?

The Yeezy Boost 350 V2 gains the most money per pair

The ten shoe lines with the biggest gain per pair: typical retail price and typical resale price (US$).

+$209: One line is far ahead

The Yeezy Boost 350 V2 retails for $220 and typically resells for $429.

It is only 20 of the 11,848 models, but they sold 138,798 pairs, about 10% of all pairs sold in the year to August 2020.

$80 or less: After the top four, the gains are modest

Nike and Jordan lines fill eight of the ten places. The Kobe 1 Protro ($184), Ultra Boost 1.0 ($138) and SB Dunk Low ($110) follow the Yeezy.

The other six lines gain $67 to $80 per pair, and five of them are Jordans.

Final takeaways

A few countries, a few companies, a few shoes

The same pattern repeats at every step, from the factory to the collector.

1

A few countries make the shoes

Five countries ship 74% of the world's athletic shoes, mostly from Asia to Europe and North America. That is globalisation in one product: made in a few places, bought everywhere, and production keeps moving, with China's share falling from 46.8% to 31.6% as Vietnam's rose from 6.6% to 23.6%.

2

A few companies earn the money, but none is safe

Nike and adidas earn 63% of the revenue of the eight companies followed here. Yet Nike's revenue fell 9.8% and then stalled, ANTA doubled its sales, and Nike left the S&P 100 in September 2026.

3

A few shoes make the collectors' money

Most models resell near or below retail. Even inside Nike, 52.5% of models lose money while 18.6% resell at least 50% above retail. The winners are often star names: five of the ten most profitable lines are Jordans, one is Kobe's and one is Kanye West's Yeezy. Collaborations hold a premium too (+10% against −7.5%), which suggests celebrity power adds value that the shoe alone does not.
